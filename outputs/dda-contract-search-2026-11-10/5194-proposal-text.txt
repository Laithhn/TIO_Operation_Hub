3.10 DEFINITION AND MEASUREMENT OF MTBF 
As a failure rate degree for an item (LRU or SRU) during the period of useful life of this item 
the MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) will be chosen. Phases of early failures or late 
failures at end of life are not included in the period of useful life and there for not reflected by 
the MTBF. 
The MTBF is a statistical average value that indicates a period without a failure during a 
period of normal use under normal functional and environmental conditions.  
Measuring a MTBF during real conditions shall follow the subsequent rules:  
• For a MTBF examination items can be observed that are used within the 
VITRONIC functional specification only.  
• As a MTBF relevant failure all events will be counted, where the failure cause 
can be clearly attributed to an item and also be reproduced.  
• The following failures are not counted as MTBF relevant failures:  
o Failures as a result of preventive maintenance  
o Degradation failures  
o Aging failures  
o Wear-out-failures and all failures in conjunction with a swap of a wear 
and tear part or a consumable.  
o Failures as a result of not realized preventive maintenance  
o Failures as a result of not realized rebuilding and/or refresh 
 
3.11 INITIAL SPARE PART INVENTORY 
The initial dimensioning of a spare part inventory will be based on the estimated minimal MTBF 
of assets. Because of different MTBF -values for LRUs and SRUs, different spare part 
inventories will result. The intent is that the initial spare part inventory will be adequate for Go-
Live-Phases with normal operation. For dimensioning of the spare part inventory, we assume 
furthermore that there are two repair cycles. On the one hand there will be a repair cycle 
between customer and VITRONIC and on the other, a repair cycle between VITRONIC and 
our suppliers. In addition, we expect that during the period of useful life of an asset the average 
repair time per involved supply chain will be four (4) weeks without transport and six (6) weeks 
including transport. For equipment with special needs regarding the availability and/or repair 
time we recommend on average basis an initial spare part inventory of 2% LRUs for swap 
purposes on site and 2% SRUs for repair purposes in a repair Centre as well as an operation 
conditions adequate number of consumables and wear and tear parts. To retain an operational 
spare part inventory, it will be necessary to carry out recommended preventive maintenance 
and to dispatch and transport defect items to VITRONIC repair center facility immediately after 
swap on site.  
If the number of spare parts will be lower as recommended or if there will be a need of 
additional spare parts in case of numerous failures, the spare part inventory will need to be

6. CALL PRIORITIES  
A system of service call priorities is used by the Company. Any of the call priorities defined 
below can be set by the Customer when reporting a fault. However, the default used is as 
follows:  
When placing the service call, the Customer will be asked if there is a major system impact. If 
the answer is YES, a P1 will be allocated. If the answer is NO, a default P2 will be allocated, 
or if more appropriate, a P3 or P4 as defined below:  
6.1 PRIORITY P1 (CRITICAL)  
A Priority ONE call is used for system faults where there is a major impact on normal operation 
of all the systems. The Company will immediately begin work on a P1 call, with the immediate 
goal of restoring normal operation to the system via a fault correction or a satisfactory work -
around. Misuse of this priority affects the ability of the Company to respond to genui ne P1 
calls.  
6.2 PRIORITY P2 (URGENT)  
Priority TWO calls are used for system faults where normal system operation is affected to 
some degree and a satisfactory work -around is not available. The Company will use best 
endeavors to respond to the call in the times stated in Response Times section  of this 
document. In most cases, it is normal for the Company to respond in faster times than those 
stated.  
6.3 PRIORITY P3 (STANDARD)  
Priority THREE calls are used for system faults where a fault was detected, but normal 
operation is not affected. This priority of call is the default for all service calls which do not 
involve a hardware related fault. For software related issues, this priority allows for work to be 
scheduled as part of a planned maintenance update.  
6.4 PRIORITY P4 (ENHANCEMENT REQUESTS)  
Priority FOUR calls are used when an enhancement request is made for potential modifications 
to System Software.
