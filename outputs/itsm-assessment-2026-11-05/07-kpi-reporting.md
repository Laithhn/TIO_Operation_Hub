# KPI and reporting assessment — limited observed scope

Assessment date: 5 November 2026. Observations are limited to the two tested sessions, visible dashboard definitions, report-library descriptions, a team-workload preview and one case report-builder sample. The Manager's library listed 13 reports, including SLA compliance, commitment performance, case aging and backlog, team workload, and client health. No customer-specific figures are reproduced, and no KPI calculation has been independently reconciled to underlying records. The Manager saw contractual SLA dashboards; the Engineer was denied dashboard, reporting and notifications in the tested session. The user confirmed Engineers should receive notifications, but contract and finance notifications must be excluded under the user-stated rule; reporting access still requires a role-scope decision.

The Manager also refreshed a case report-builder preview filtered to UAT case CS-1297. It returned exactly one row, showing Closed, the Engineer owner and No SLA, consistent with the test case. This verifies that one sample in the preview only; other report outputs and calculations remain unverified. No report was exported, saved or scheduled. Access review showed `Notifications:View` and `Reporting:View` not included / not granted, and `Dashboard:View` not included due to a missing prerequisite. `Cases:View` and `Cases:Maintain` were included through the Operation Engineer role. These results are consistent with the observed UI access, but do not prove enforcement on every endpoint.

The live dashboard defines response and resolution rates separately as **met ÷ (met + breached)** and defines its overall case measure using cases with both obligations completed and both met. A headline combines obligations, so it can differ numerically from the overall case measure. The display also distinguishes operational due-date adherence from contractual SLA. These are observed definitions, not proof that the underlying calculations or source data are correct. The [PeopleCert service-level-management overview](https://www.peoplecert.org/browse-certifications/it-governance-and-service-management/ITIL-1/itil-4-practitioner-service-level-management-3867) provides practice context; targets and definitions should follow the service agreement and business purpose.

## Verification and prioritization

1. Agree a KPI dictionary: intended decision, data owner, record population, event timestamps, clock basis, exclusions, numerator, denominator, timezone, refresh time and permissions.
2. Reconcile a small set of safe, known records through the case, dashboard and report views, including no measured cases, pending and breached obligations, waiting, reopen, support-hour and daylight-saving boundaries. Confirm client-specific holiday configuration and calculation against the relevant contracts.
3. Review the obligation-versus-case SLA labels and the zero-record escalation count before relying on management interpretation.
4. Verify Engineers receive their user-requested notifications and have a usable personal work path; verify the exclusion of contract and finance notifications before granting access. The observed page denial alone does not prove delivery failed.

Match the deployed build and test with known records before treating those details as deployed facts. **Controlled SLA fixture, 5 November 2026:** The non-production test client UAT_SLA_20261105, its project, [contract CT-1005](https://nb01994.vitronic.de/contracts/5) and [case CS-1298](https://nb01994.vitronic.de/cases/9498) used support hours 09:00–17:00 every day in Asia/Dubai, a 40-support-hour response and resolution target, and customer-wait pause off. The contract notes described fictional 6 November as an excluded holiday. From the displayed clock start of 5 November 10:27, the application displayed 10 November 10:27 for both deadlines. Independent arithmetic gives 10 November 10:27 when 6 November is counted, and 11 November 10:27 when it is excluded. The note-only exclusion was therefore not applied in this fixture. No holiday-date input appeared in the inspected contract form; a free-text note is not a configured holiday. The client edit form showed no holiday/calendar field, and Administration searches for “holiday” and “calendar” each returned zero pages. This confirms a gap in the inspected UI against the user-requested client calendar, not that configuration is impossible through every route; uninspected routes and configured holiday enforcement remain unverified. The client, project and contract remain active and the case remains open; these persistent test records can affect non-production metrics.
